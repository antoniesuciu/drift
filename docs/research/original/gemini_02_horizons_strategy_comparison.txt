Executive Summary: The Strongest ConclusionsAn empirical evaluation of short-horizon algorithmic trading (5 to 60 minutes) for an independent European retail trader reveals that viable profitability is governed primarily by the ratio of expected price excursion over the holding period to fixed and proportional transaction friction, rather than by machine-learning complexity. Many strategies published in academic literature fail in live retail trading because they omit exchange clearing fees, minimum ticket floors, bid-ask spread crossing, and realistic limit-order fill probabilities.   The strongest evidence-backed conclusions of this study are:Optimal Market and Instrument: CME Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100 (MNQ) and Micro E-mini S&P 500 (MES) futures, followed secondarily by high-beta US individual equities (accessible without the $25,000 Pattern Day Trader restriction following FINRA's April–June 2026 rule abolition, requiring only a $2,000 margin floor with 2x Regulation T leverage).   Infeasibility of Short-Horizon Spot Cryptocurrency for Retail Takers: Spot Bitcoin (BTC) and Ethereum (ETH) are mathematically unviable at 5-to-30-minute horizons on standard retail fee tiers. Following Kraken Pro's July 9, 2026 fee restructure, entry-level spot traders pay 0.40% maker / 0.80% taker (80 to 160 bps round trip), while Interactive Brokers' crypto partnership via Paxos/ZeroHash charges 0.12% to 0.18% with a $1.75 minimum per order (35 to 200 bps round trip on small tickets). Meanwhile, Binance—despite offering lower 0.10% spot (0.075% in BNB) and 0.02%/0.05% futures fees—has lacked MiCA authorization for the EU since July 1, 2026, exposing European retail traders to severe regulatory and fiat-rail risk. Short-term crypto strategies are only viable if executed via regulated derivatives (such as Kraken Pro Multi-M Futures at 0.02% maker / 0.05% taker) or zero-maker-fee spot pairs like Kraken's MiCA-compliant USDG (0.00% maker / 0.01% taker).   Optimal Holding Horizon: Horizon B (15–30 minutes) and Horizon C (30–60 minutes) strongly dominate Horizon A (5–15 minutes). On MES, an all-in round-trip market-order trade costs $1.22 in hard fees ($0.50 IBKR commission + $0.70 CME fee + $0.02 NFA fee) plus $2.50 in spread and slippage (2 ticks), totaling $3.72 (2.98 ticks). Over a 5-minute window, this fixed friction consumes 25% to 50% of the typical bar range; over a 15-to-60-minute window, session-opening and macro volatility expansions routinely span 15 to 60 ticks, reducing friction drag to a manageable fraction of gross expectancy.   Optimal Strategy and Modeling Paradigm: Session-anchored Volatility / Opening Range Breakouts (ORB) and Intraday Momentum Continuation at the 15-to-60-minute horizon have the strongest empirical support. Simple statistical rules or regularized gradient-boosted decision trees (LightGBM / XGBoost) used in a Meta-Labeling configuration (predicting the probability of hitting triple-barrier profit/loss targets net of costs) reliably outperform deep neural networks (LSTMs, Transformers) and Reinforcement Learning, which overfit low-signal-to-noise intraday financial data.   Infrastructure Realism: Unlike sub-second high-frequency trading, a 15-to-60-minute strategy does not require geographic colocation for latency reasons—a 60 ms transatlantic delay from Europe to New York/Chicago represents less than 0.01% of a 15-minute bar. Local execution on an Ubuntu workstation is sufficient for initial research and paper validation, while a low-cost ($20–$30/month) VPS is justified at live deployment strictly for network uptime and fault-tolerant position management when holding leveraged futures.   2. Quantitative Cost Examples and Break-Even Price MovementsTo avoid the common backtesting error of assuming 100% passive limit-order fills at zero spread cost, we model two realistic execution regimes:   Taker / Stop-Entry Regime (100% Marketable Orders): The strategy crosses the bid-ask spread on both entry and exit (incurring $1.0 \times \text{Spread}$ round-trip cost) plus $1.0 \text{ tick}$ (or realistic bps) of adverse slippage during volatility breakouts, plus two taker fees.   Blended Passive-Entry / Taker-Exit Regime (Realistic Limit Orders): Because passive limit orders suffer from adverse selection (orders fill with 100% certainty when price moves through your limit into a loss, but often go unfilled when price bounces away in your favor), we model a realistic blended execution where entry achieves a passive maker fill 60% of the time (earning half-spread) and requires crossing the spread as a taker 40% of the time, while exit (stop-loss or time barrier) executes as a 100% marketable taker order with 0.5 ticks of slippage. Choosing the Right Prediction Target: Why Meta-Labeling WinsTraining a model to predict raw future directional returns ($r_{t, t+h} = \frac{P_{t+h}}{P_t} - 1$) via Mean Squared Error fails because financial returns are heteroskedastic (a 20 bps move during a quiet 12:00 CET hour is a 3-sigma event, whereas a 20 bps move during a 14:30 CET CPI release is noise) and completely ignores path-dependent stopouts.   Instead, evidence strongly supports Triple-Barrier Labeling paired with Meta-Labeling (Lopez de Prado, 2018):   Primary Model (Rule-Based): A deterministic rule with economic rationale (e.g., a 15-minute Opening Range Breakout on MNQ) proposes a trade direction $s_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ at timestamp $t_{i,0}$.   Triple-Barrier Target: Set three dynamic barriers scaled by rolling intraday volatility $\sigma_t$:An upper profit-taking barrier at $P_{t_{i,0}} (1 + \alpha \sigma_t)$ (where $\alpha \sigma_t$ significantly exceeds round-trip transaction costs $c$).A lower stop-loss barrier at $P_{t_{i,0}} (1 - \beta \sigma_t)$.A vertical expiration barrier at $t_{i,1} = t_{i,0} + \Delta t$ (e.g., 30 or 60 minutes).   Meta-Label Target ($y_i \in \{0, 1\}$): Label the trade $y_i = 1$ if and only if the primary signal realizes a net profit after deducting realistic round-trip transaction costs and 1 tick of slippage before hitting the stop-loss or time barrier; otherwise $y_i = 0$.   Meta-Model (LightGBM / Logistic Regression): Train a shallow classifier on contextual regime features (opening range width relative to 20-day ATR, TBBO bid-ask spread stability, VIX/volatility term structure, volume participation ratio, time-of-day) to predict the probability $p_i = P(y_i = 1)$. The system accepts the trade only when $p_i > p_{\text{threshold}}$ and sizes the position proportionally to calibrated edge.   7. Reproducible Validation MethodologyTo prevent deploying an overfitted backtest to live capital, every candidate 5–60 minute strategy must pass an eight-stage validation protocol before paper trading:Pre-Registration in Obsidian: Prior to running a backtest, record the exact economic hypothesis, entry/exit equations, target market, and parameter bounds in Obsidian. Log every trial number $N_{\text{trials}}$ so selection bias can be mathematically penalized.   Causal Timestamp Verification (Lookahead Prevention): Verify that all 1-minute and 5-minute bars are timestamped at interval close (bars_timestamp_on_close=True in Databento) so a bar covering 15:30:00–15:35:00 CET only emits its on_bar() event at 15:35:00.000000000. Run an automated Freqtrade-style lookahead test: run the backtest on full dataset $[0, T]$, then truncate the dataset at the midpoint $[0, T/2]$ and verify that 100% of entry/exit timestamps, quantities, and prices in $[0, T/2 - \epsilon]$ match bit-for-bit.   Chronological Holdout & Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation (CPCV): Reserve the most recent 20% of historical data as an untouched chronological out-of-sample (OOS) vault. On the remaining 80% development data, evaluate hyperparameters and ML meta-labels using CPCV ($N=6$ groups, $k=2$ test splits $\implies 15$ splits yielding 5 distinct full-length OOS backtest paths), applying strict Purging (removing training labels whose $[t_{i,0}, t_{i,1}]$ horizon overlaps test windows) and 1% Embargoing.   Realistic Friction Injection: Apply full broker commission + exchange/NFA fees ($1.22/RT on MES/MNQ; $0.35 min + $0.0030/sh take fee on US equities) plus full bid-ask spread crossing.   Adverse Slippage Sensitivity Stress-Test: Re-run the simulation under three slippage multipliers: 1.0x baseline (1 tick RT), 2.0x stress (2 ticks RT = $2.50 extra on MES, $1.00 extra on MNQ), and 1-bar execution delay. If net expectancy turns negative under 2.0x slippage, reject the strategy.   Multiple-Testing Correction (Deflated Sharpe Ratio): Compute the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) (Lopez de Prado), which adjusts the observed Sharpe ratio for non-Normal return skewness $\hat{\gamma}_3$, kurtosis $\hat{\gamma}_4$, track-record length $T$, and the total number of independent trials $N_{\text{trials}}$ tested during research. Require $\text{DSR} > 0.95$ (statistically significant above the expected maximum Sharpe of $N_{\text{trials}}$ random noise configurations).   Sub-Period Regime Decomposition: Partition OOS performance across distinct macro and volatility regimes: (a) Bull Low-Vol, (b) Bear High-Vol / VIX > 25, (c) Sideways Chop, and (d) High-Rate vs. Low-Rate environments.
